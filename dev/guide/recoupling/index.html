Recoupling Coefficients

Kiwi computes explicit representation matrices, Clebsch–Gordan coefficients and recoupling symbols (F/6j, R, A, B, 1j, 2j, 3j) for every compact simple Lie algebra, including tensor products with outer multiplicity. This page states the conventions precisely: individual coefficients are only meaningful together with them.

Quick tour

using Kiwi
su3 = SU(3)
a = Irrep(su3, 1, 1)                     # 8
nsymbol(a, a, a)                         # 8 appears twice in 8 ⊗ 8
2
C = fusion_tensor(a, a, a)               # ⟨8 i; 8 j | 8 k; μ⟩, size (8, 8, 8, 2)
size(C)
(8, 8, 8, 2)
F = fsymbol(a, a, a, a, a, a)            # F^{888}_8[8, 8], size (2, 2, 2, 2)
size(F)
(2, 2, 2, 2)
E, Fm, H = generators(Irrep(SU(2), 2))  # spin 1: E = J₊, F = J₋, H = 2J_z
Matrix(Fm[1])
3×3 Matrix{Float64}:
 0.0      0.0      0.0
 1.41421  0.0      0.0
 0.0      1.41421  0.0

Conventions

Bases of irreducible modules

  • Weights of $V(λ)$ are ordered by depth (the height of $λ − μ$) and, within a depth, by decreasing Dynkin labels. The highest weight comes first, and basis_weights lists the weight of every basis vector.
  • Lowering basis (basis = :lowering, the default, all algebras). The candidates spanning $V(λ)_μ$ are the vectors $F_i b$ for $i = 1, …, n$ in turn and $b$ running over the basis of $V(λ)_{μ+α_i}$. The basis is the first independent subfamily of candidates, chosen greedily in this order, and then Gram–Schmidt orthonormalised in the same order with respect to the contravariant form ($⟨v_λ, v_λ⟩ = 1$, $F_i^† = E_i$). Each basis vector therefore has positive overlap with the candidate it came from. For $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ this is the Condon–Shortley basis, with $F = J_-$, $E = J_+$ and $H = 2J_z$.
  • Gelfand–Tsetlin basis (basis = :gelfand_tsetlin, $\mathfrak{su}(N)$ only): the standard GT basis with nonnegative raising-operator matrix elements, the same basis as in SUNRepresentations.jl. Within a weight space, patterns are ordered by decreasing rows $(m_{N-1}, …, m_1)$.
  • Generators are the Chevalley generators $E_i, F_i, H_i$, with $[E_i, F_j] = δ_{ij} H_i$ and $[H_i, E_j] = ⟨α_j, α_i^∨⟩ E_j$. In both bases $E_i = F_i^T$.
  • With T = Rational{BigInt} the module is built in exact arithmetic, in the unnormalised lowering basis (with its Gram matrices). Its Gram–Schmidt orthonormalisation is exactly the floating-point lowering basis.

Clebsch–Gordan coefficients and the multiplicity convention

fusion_tensor(a, b, c)[i, j, k, μ] = ⟨a i; b j | c k; μ⟩ uses TensorKit's layout. For each $μ$ the map $e_k ↦ \sum_{ij} C[i,j,k,μ]\, e_i ⊗ e_j$ is an isometric intertwiner $V_c → V_a ⊗ V_b$.

The intertwiners are fixed by their highest-weight vectors $X$. Let $K$ be the space of highest-weight vectors of weight $c$ in $V_a ⊗ V_b$, and $f_1, f_2, …$ the basis of $V_b(c − a)$. The basis $X_1, …, X_N$ is the Gram–Schmidt orthonormalisation of the projections $Π_K(v_a ⊗ f_j)$, taken for $j = 1, 2, …$ in order and skipping those that depend on earlier ones. So $⟨X_μ, v_a ⊗ f_{j_μ}⟩ > 0$. In the multiplicity-free case, the first nonzero coefficient of $X$ along $v_a ⊗ V_b(c − a)$ is positive. For $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ this is the Condon–Shortley convention $⟨j_1 j_1; j_2\, (J − j_1) | J J⟩ > 0$, and Kiwi reproduces the Racah formula.

fusion_tensor(a, b, c; sparse = true) returns the same coefficients as a sparse $(\dim a \cdot \dim b) × (\dim c \cdot N)$ matrix.

F- and R-symbols

Both follow TensorKitSectors.jl:

  • $F^{abc}_d[e, f]$ is an array of size $(N^{ab}_e, N^{ec}_d, N^{bc}_f, N^{af}_d)$ relating $((a ⊗ b → e) ⊗ c → d)$ and $(a ⊗ (b ⊗ c → f) → d)$:

    \[F[μ,ν,κ,λ] = \sum C^{af}_d[i,n,1,λ]\, C^{bc}_f[j,l,n,κ]\, C^{ab}_e[i,j,m,μ]\, C^{ec}_d[m,l,1,ν].\]

  • $R^{ab}_c[μ, ν] = \sum_{ij} C^{ba}_c[j,i,1,ν]\, C^{ab}_c[i,j,1,μ]$.

Kiwi evaluates these contractions on highest-weight vectors (index 1), without forming the full Clebsch–Gordan tensors. For $\mathfrak{su}(2)$,

\[F^{j_1 j_2 j_3}_j[j_{12}, j_{23}] = (-1)^{j_1+j_2+j_3+j}\sqrt{(2j_{12}+1)(2j_{23}+1)} \begin{Bmatrix} j_1 & j_2 & j_{12} \\ j_3 & j & j_{23}\end{Bmatrix}, \qquad R^{j_1 j_2}_J = (-1)^{j_1+j_2-J}.\]

1j, 2j and 3j symbols

  • 2j symbol. $Z_a = \sqrt{\dim a}\; C^{a\bar a}_1[:, :, 1, 1]$ (twoj) is a real orthogonal matrix (rows: basis of $a$, columns: basis of $\bar a$). The multiplicity convention gives $Z_a[\mathrm{hw}(a), \mathrm{lw}(\bar a)] > 0$. For $\mathfrak{su}(2)$, $Z_j[m, m'] = (-1)^{j-m} δ_{m', -m}$.
  • 1j symbol. By Schur's lemma $Z_{\bar a} = ϰ_a Z_a^T$ with $ϰ_a = ±1$ (onej). For self-conjugate $a$, $ϰ_a$ is the Frobenius–Schur indicator ($+1$ real, $-1$ pseudoreal). For $a ≠ \bar a$, $ϰ_a = ϰ_{\bar a}$ depends on the convention. In all cases $ϰ_a$ equals TensorKit's Frobenius–Schur phase $\operatorname{sign} F^{a\bar a a}_a[1, 1]$. This is what makes TensorKit's line-bending A- and B-symbols (asymbol, bsymbol) agree whether they are computed from F-symbols or from fusion tensors.
  • 3j symbol. $(a\,b\,c)[i,j,k,μ] = \sum_m C^{ab}_{\bar c}[i,j,m,μ]\, Z_c[k,m] / \sqrt{\dim c}$ (threej) is an invariant of $V_a ⊗ V_b ⊗ V_c$ normalised to $\sum |·|^2 = 1$. For $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ it is related to Wigner's symbol by $\begin{pmatrix} j_1 & j_2 & j_3 \\ m_1 & m_2 & m_3 \end{pmatrix}_{\text{Wigner}} = (-1)^{j_1-j_2-j_3}\,(j_1\,j_2\,j_3)_{\text{Kiwi}}$.

Exact values and the rational gauge

With T = Rational{BigInt} everything is computed in exact arithmetic. Unitary F-, R-, A- and B-symbols are returned as SqrtRational values $s\sqrt{q}$ (with or without outer multiplicity):

q, qb = Irrep(su3, 1, 0), Irrep(su3, 0, 1)        # 3, 3̄
fsymbol(q, q, qb, q, qb, a; T = Rational{BigInt})  # F^{3 3 3̄}_3[3̄, 8]
1×1×1×1 Array{SqrtRational, 4}:
[:, :, 1, 1] =
 √(2/3)

Rational gauge

For computations that must stay rational (for example modulo a prime), every symbol has a raw form, normalized = false, that takes no square roots:

  • the module bases are the unnormalised lowering bases, with Gram matrices given by the Shapovalov form;
  • the vertices (intertwiners) $X_1, …, X_N$ of $c → a ⊗ b$ are mutually orthogonal but unnormalised. They are the unnormalised Gram–Schmidt vectors of the multiplicity convention above, which are canonical rational functions of the labels. Their squared norms $n_μ = ⟨X_μ, X_μ⟩$ (the theta values) are returned with every raw symbol;
  • a raw symbol is the same contraction as the unitary one, evaluated on these vertices. The unitary symbol is the raw one divided by the square root of the product of the norms² of the vertices involved, with $\sqrt{\dim}$ factors for the bending symbols.
call (normalized = false)returnsunitary value
fsymbol(a,b,c,d,e,f; T)(F, norms = (nᵃᵇₑ, nᵉᶜ_d, nᵇᶜ_f, nᵃᶠ_d))$F[μνκλ]/\sqrt{n_μ n_ν n_κ n_λ}$
rsymbol(a,b,c; T)(R, norms = (nᵃᵇ_c, nᵇᵃ_c))$R[μν]/\sqrt{n_μ n_ν}$
twoj(a; T)(X, norm2, gram_a, gram_abar) (the cup)$\sqrt{\dim a}\, R_a X R_{\bar a}^T/\sqrt{n}$, $G = R^TR$
asymbol(a,b,c; T)(A, norms, scale2)$\sqrt{\text{scale2}}\,A[μν]/\sqrt{n_{\rm cup} n_{\rm unit} n_μ n_ν}$
bsymbol(a,b,c; T)(B, norms, scale2)$\sqrt{\text{scale2}}\,B[μν]/\sqrt{n_μ n_ν n_{\rm cup} n_{\rm unit}}$
fusion_tensor(a,b,c; T)(C, gram_a, gram_b, gram_c, norms2)$(R_a ⊗ R_b)\, C_μ R_c^{-1}/\sqrt{n_μ}$

The 1j symbol onej(a) is a sign computed once, exactly, and cached per irrep.

Prime fields

The same code runs over Kiwi.Fp{P} ($P < 2^{62}$ prime), returning the residues of the raw rational data. Every choice the construction makes (the basis of each weight space, and the projections that fix each multiplicity basis) is decided modulo the reference prime $2^{61} − 1$ and reused for every other prime. So all primes reduce the same rational numbers. A prime that divides one of the Gram determinants involved raises an error instead of returning residues of a different gauge.

P = Kiwi.Fp{2305843009213693951}                    # 2⁶¹ − 1
fsymbol(a, a, a, a, a, a; T = P, normalized = false).F[1, 1, 1, 1]
1067861518044741821 mod 2305843009213693951

TensorKit

After using TensorKitSectors (or TensorKit), tensorkit_sector(g) is a sector type whose topological data are Kiwi's, so tensors with the symmetry of any compact simple Lie group can be used in TensorKit:

using Kiwi, TensorKitSectors
I = tensorkit_sector(Kiwi.SU(3))
s = tensorkit_sector(Irrep(Kiwi.SU(3), 1, 1))
Fsymbol(s, s, s, s, s, s)

Kiwi's sectors pass TensorKitSectors' own test suite: pentagon, hexagon, triangle, unitarity, and the fusion-tensor/F-symbol consistency of the A, B and Frobenius–Schur data.
